Definitions & How It Works — a plain-language reference for anyone new to these reports

1.What this suite is

Every Pizza Hut franchise restaurant gets a 5-Star score each month, measuring performance across five operational areas. That raw score, on its own, doesn't tell anyone what to do next. This suite turns it into purpose-built reports — one for each audience — so Leadership, Directors, FOPs, OAs, and Training all get the view that answers their specific questions, without needing a database, a login, or a BI tool.

Every report is a single file you open in a browser. All of them are built from the same underlying data at the same time, so a number means the same thing wherever you see it — a store's overall score on the Zone Scorecard is the exact same number you'd see for that store on the Franchisee Dashboard.

The core idea A store's overall score is a weighted average, but the lowest of its five components — its binding constraint (see §5) — is what actually tells someone what to coach on. Every report leads with binding constraints, not just overall scores, because that's the actionable layer.

3.Tiers

Every store is sorted into one of three tiers based on its overall 5-Star score:

TierNameScore rangeMeaning
Tier 1Bootcampbelow 2.5 ★Needs intervention
Tier 2Rising Star2.5 ★ – 4.0 ★On track, room to grow
Tier 3Top Tier4.0 ★ and aboveExcellent — protect and replicate

Most places use a store's current single month score to set its tier. A few features (Boot Camp targeting, zone coverage tracking) instead use the trailing 3-month average, specifically so one unusually good or bad month doesn't flip a store's tier back and forth. Where that happens, the report says so right next to the number.

4.Store status

Status is a separate flag from tier, built from how a store's score has trended over consecutive months — not just where it sits this month. It mirrors the Brand Standards Manual's default language.

StatusPlain meaningThe actual ruleWho acts
DefaultingCrossed the contractual default line 3 consecutive months under 2.0 ★, or 4 months under 2.0 ★ in any recent window FOP escalates — formal improvement plan
At RiskOne step from defaulting Exactly 2 consecutive months under 2.0 ★ (and not already Defaulting) FOP/OA steps in before it becomes contractual
T1 WatchIn Bootcamp tier, but not yet failing Currently scoring 2.0–2.5 ★, and not Defaulting or At Risk Monitor — get ahead of it
OKEverything else—Business as usual

A quick way to remember the difference from Tier: Tier is "how good is this store right now," Status is "is this store failing badly enough, for long enough, that it's a contract problem." A store can be Tier 1 (under 2.5★) for years without ever being Defaulting, as long as it never drops under 2.0★ for that many consecutive months.

5.Binding constraint

Every store gets five component scores (next section). Its binding constraint is simply whichever of the five is lowest that month. If a tier's stores are mostly bound on Speed, that's where coaching time should go — not Brand, not Win Score. Every "what's binding" chart across the suite is built from this one idea, aggregated up: for a group of stores, it's the % of stores where each component is the one holding them back.

6.The five scorecard components

A store's Overall 5-Star score is a weighted blend of five components. The weights are fixed and don't change month to month:

ComponentWeightWhat it measures
Win Score35%Customer satisfaction — surveys, aggregator ratings (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub), and public reviews (Google, Yelp)
Speed30%Delivery stores: % of orders out the door under 18 minutes. Non-delivery stores: % with production time under 15 minutes
Hutbot On-Time20%% of required in-store routines/checklists completed on time
Brand7.5%Adherence to Pizza Hut brand standards (visit-based)
FSCC7.5%Food safety performance and compliance

Each component is first graded on its own 1–5 star scale, using these cutoffs (for the two with public thresholds):

Component1 ★2 ★3 ★4 ★5 ★
Win Scoreunder 49%49–60%60–66%66–71%71%+
Speedunder 40%40–60%60–70%70–80%80%+
Hutbot On-Timeunder 80%80–85%85–90%90–95%95%+

Brand and FSCC stars come from visit/audit results rather than a single percentage, so they don't have the same simple 5-bucket cutoff table. A store's Overall 5-Star is then the weighted sum of all five stars using the percentages above — it isn't recomputed by the reports, it comes pre-blended from the scoring system.

7.How numbers roll up — the one rule

Whenever you see a number for a group of stores (an Area, a Franchisee, a Zone, a Region, or the whole company), it follows one rule:

The rule Never average the averages — sum the parts, then calculate.

For example, to get a Zone's "% of deliveries under 30 minutes," the report adds up every store's count of on-time deliveries, adds up every store's total delivery count, and only then divides — it never averages each store's individual percentage. Averaging percentages directly would let a tiny 50-order store count exactly as much as an 800-order store, distorting the real picture. The one place this doesn't apply is a handful of scorecard star ratings themselves, which are volume-weighted averages (bigger stores count more) rather than raw sums, since a star rating isn't built from a simple numerator/denominator the way a delivery percentage is.

9.Boot Camp & Rising Star workshops

OAs run hands-on workshops to help struggling stores improve: Boot Camp for Tier 1 stores, Rising Star for Tier 2 stores — usually grouped by DMA and Area Coach so the right people are in the room together. Every workshop-related chart in the suite is answering the same question: did the stores that attended improve more than similar stores that didn't?

The four checkpoints

Each workshop is measured at a baseline (the store's score right before attending) and then 30, 60, and 90 days after. A store only counts as "measurable" once it's reached the 30-day mark — before that, it's counted as "had a Boot Camp" but isn't yet averaged into the improvement numbers, so a store that attended last week can't drag down (or inflate) results before it's had time to show anything.

Control group

To know whether a workshop actually helped, each attending group is compared to a control group: stores in the same tier, at the same point in time, that did not attend. If attendees improved more than their tier-matched peers who skipped it, that's the workshop working.

Coverage

The Zone Scorecard's Boot Camp donut shows what share of a zone's current Tier 1 stores have had, or have scheduled, a Boot Camp — "Had," "Scheduled," and "Need" are the three slices. Getting every current Tier 1 store covered is the goal; the worklist underneath the donut is exactly the stores still needing one.

10.Zones — 15 vs. 24, and the toggle

Pizza Hut's field organization is moving from 15 zones (the long-standing structure) to 24 zones (new, smaller territories, several carved out of the original 15 as new Operations Assistants are hired). Both the Zone Scorecards page and the Leadership Summary's Zone Ranking table let you toggle between the two views of the exact same underlying store data — nothing about a store's score changes, only how stores are grouped and labeled.

  • 24 Zones is the current, live org structure — every OA position shows up, including ones recently hired into but not yet handling stores (shown with 0 stores until that happens), and any position still vacant shows as OPEN.
  • 15 Zones is the legacy structure. Because new 24-zone positions didn't exist under the old structure, they simply don't appear in the 15-zone view — that's expected, not a gap.

15 Zones is the default view on both pages; switch to 24 Zones with the toggle at the top.

11.Glossary of roles & terms

OA (Operations Assistant)
Runs a Zone. Trains shoulder-to-shoulder in the markets that need the most help and runs Boot Camp / Rising Star workshops.
FOP (Franchise Operations Partner)
Manages the relationship with a set of franchisees — not individual stores. Needs to know, before a franchisee call, which stores are in trouble and why.
Director
Oversees a group of FOPs. Rolls up to a whole territory's worth of franchisees and stores.
Franchisee
The business owner who owns and operates one or more restaurants.
Area
A grouping of stores under one Area Coach, used for Boot Camp/Rising Star targeting. Not the same as "OA" — a store's Area Coach and its OA are usually different people with different jobs, which is a common mix-up.
DMA
Designated Market Area — a standard media/geography grouping (e.g. "Kansas City") used to group stores by local market.
Region
A broader geography grouping, above DMA.
Zone
The territory one OA is responsible for — see §10 for the 15-vs-24 distinction.
Chained Store ID
The unique number identifying one restaurant, used to look up a store across every report.
Concept
Whether a store is a delivery or non-delivery restaurant — several metrics (like Speed) are defined differently for each.

12.Where the numbers come from

Every number in these reports comes from the company's live systems — there's no manual data entry behind any score, tier, or status. Reports are regenerated periodically (typically once a new month's scores have landed), and the five files always reflect the same snapshot of data, generated together, so nothing can disagree with itself across reports.
